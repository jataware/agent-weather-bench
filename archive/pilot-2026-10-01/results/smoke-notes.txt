ACCORD development smoke — 1 October 2026

Seven agent attempts: three initial Fable 5.1 arms, three fresh-conversation
follow-ups with each arm's retained files, and one Haiku 4.5 scratch initial.
Eight Sonnet 5.5 judge calls: seven final judgments plus one superseded judgment
before a checker correction. All final judgments cite preserved evidence.

Observed outcome
Rhiza initial: 11/12 checks; usable fitted predictor. Scratch recovered on its
follow-up: 11/12. Both missed answer.json in offline solve.py replay despite
reproducing numerical arrays and figures. ACCORD did not complete either attempt.
Haiku: 6/12; saved files but failed scientific/output checks. No attempt earned
full completion. No model capability threshold or ACCORD superiority established.
Rhiza read a skill document but did not run catalog scripts. ACCORD inspected
library documentation/API signatures but its retained calibration code used generic
Python. Availability and substantive library use are different measurements.

Reuse
Rhiza's model.nc hash was unchanged in the follow-up. Its original frozen predictor
processed the second private forecast batch in 1.489 seconds including Docker
startup/cleanup, with no LLM calls. The fresh-conversation follow-up cost $4.19835
versus $4.28032 initially: little token-dollar saving despite less elapsed time.
Scratch's follow-up was recovery from incomplete work, not reuse of a working fit.
No fresh-workspace follow-up controls or repeated seeds were run.

Scientific scope
Identical supplied normalized monthly ECMWF/CHIRPS fields; no acquisition scored.
Training 1993–2004, private-to-agent prediction batches 2005–06 and 2007–08.
All years had previously been inspected in development. Twelve spatial cells are
not twelve independent climates; two years cannot establish calibration. Report
RPS/RMSE/reliability separately. Rhiza's first-batch RPS beats raw/model-climate
baselines, but its second-batch RPS is worse than model-climate. No general skill
improvement follows from these numbers.

Infrastructure and evaluator history
v1/v2 were stopped for native NetCDF/HDF5 crashes and a missing shared library.
Their files, traces and costs remain under runs/smoke-v1 and smoke-v2, excluded
from comparison. The final common environment uses NetCDF3/scipy input and
h5netcdf output and passed all three arm I/O probes before v3 launched.
V3 large tool calls repeatedly hit the common 8192-output-token limit; scratch
and ACCORD consumed budgets without completing files. ACCORD's follow-up final
API request timed out at the 600-second deadline. Partial work was frozen and
scored without another agent request. Provider usage for that request is unknown.
The runner now preserves timeout artifacts through its normal completion path.
The scratch follow-up's correct training arrays initially failed because an extra
valid_fraction_min diagnostic was present. The checker now compares the required
fields, a regression test covers it, and all completed submissions were regraded.
The original check/judge record is preserved under evaluation-revisions, and the
changed submission received a new judge call. No agent artifacts were altered.
Judge citations to nested check IDs, check:ID aliases and metrics were validated
against evidence already present in their original packet. No ratings were edited.

Before a larger experiment
Review this smoke together. Fix oversized-command handling with common chunking
instructions or a reviewed response budget; make the fit/save/predict path easy
to discover; ensure solve.py recreates the entire submission contract. Freeze a
revised protocol before repeating all arms. Kenya rainfall is the next candidate
for retrieval/skills overlap; IOD needs authenticated ECDS and valid-date audits.
Keep the open-web condition separate. Do not present these smoke results as a
clean demonstration of an ACCORD advantage.
