# Numerical error bounds

The weather source is float32 cumulative mass per area in exact multiples of 1/32 mm. Differences of these values and divisions by the retained one- or two-day interval lengths are exact in binary arithmetic; a signed seven-day net total or sum of clipped daily increments remains an exact multiple of 1/32 mm. Current implementation A integrates current accumulation by endpoint subtraction and implementation B by daily increments. Both integrate the derived interval rates against exact durations.

The output tolerances allow either float32 or float64 reductions. A cell mean averages 101 members: 100 additions and one division. Bounds compare two implementations using `2 × n × 2^-24 × magnitude`, with n=101 for each cell mean, 203 for their difference and 371 for the regional mean including at most 56 cells. Magnitude 200 mm covers accepted frozen reference values and the changed-data construction. These bounds are 0.002408 mm for means, 0.004840 mm for cell changes and 0.008845 mm for regional changes. Coordinates remain exact provider labels within one float64 rounding operation at 180 degrees.

The difference identity invariant permits the sum of the two mean bounds and the change bound. The regional-range invariant permits the declared regional bound. Comparison still checks each individual output against its own reference bound. This accepts independent scalar, vectorized, float32 output and reordered-label solutions without making a scientific difference disappear. Both named pitfalls are separated by over 11,000 output bounds on every supported instance.

Both signed net native increments and clipping of tiny negative native increments are accepted. The derived interval-rate channel is integrated as delivered; it lacks daily detail within two-day intervals. These bounds concern arithmetic, not uncertainty in the meteorological forecast. The task does not assess forecast skill or claim observation-verified improvements.
