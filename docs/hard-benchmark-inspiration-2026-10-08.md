The most useful borrowing is an experiment loop around a genuine forecasting objective, with a checked attainable comparator and independent final verification. Adding more prescribed arithmetic would not reproduce the difficulty of these research benchmarks. This is primary-source research and design advice; it introduces no template, framework dependency, model call or scientific worker.

AIRS-Bench is Meta's **AI Research Science Benchmark**, [paper 2602.06855](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06855v1) and [official repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/airs-bench). It is distinct from the similarly named audio and information-retrieval AIR-Bench projects. “AI R&D benchmark” is ambiguous; the closest named match is METR's **RE-Bench**, [paper 2411.15114](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15114v1), [repository](https://github.com/METR/RE-Bench). OpenAI's **MLE-bench** and **PaperBench** are complementary interpretations, rather than aliases of RE-Bench.

| Primary evidence | Task shape, difficulty and original evidence |
| --- | --- |
| [AIRS-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06855v1) | Twenty tasks leave methods open without baseline code. Test labels and SOTA details are hidden. Runs use 24 hours, one H200 and at least ten seeds. Published SOTA is exceeded on four tasks and missed on sixteen. Fourteen model/scaffold pairs include gpt-oss20B/120B; the larger model performs better. Validation arrangements differ across harnesses. These are original results, not Luna evidence. |
| [RE-Bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15114v1) | Seven open-ended engineering environments include working starting solutions, strong references and repeatable scores. Seventy-one eight-hour expert attempts from 61 people check attainability. Hardware varies from 0–6 H100s. Scores anchor the starting solution at 0 and reference at 1. Agents lead over short budgets; humans improve more over eight hours. The authors discarded over 12 specifications and over 5 implemented candidates during development. Their difficulty dimensions include exploration, codebase complexity and feedback latency. |
| [MLE-bench paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07095v2) | Seventy-five offline Kaggle jobs require predictions as CSV, not a prescribed algorithm. A local tool checks validity without revealing test scores. Main runs allow 24 hours on one A10, 36 vCPUs and 440 GB RAM. Original AIDE medal rates were 16.9% for o1-preview and8.7% for GPT-4o; longer GPT-4o runs reached 11.8%. Its safeguards include reconstructed holdouts, rules against copying solutions, transcript review and plagiarism checks; contamination is not ruled out generally. |
| [PaperBench official release](https://openai.com/index/paperbench/) | Twenty research-paper replications use author-developed criteria for partial completion and a separately assessed automated judge. The reported best tested agent scored 21.0%. This suggests checking meaningful scientific intermediate achievements, rather than turning every report sentence into the completion gate. |

These studies do not demonstrate a cheap-model forecasting boundary. Their model generations, hardware, wall times, seeds and tasks differ from ours. “Missed SOTA” also combines incomplete implementations, weak experiments and genuinely worse scientific methods. None licenses interpreting a fifteen-minute packaging failure as inability to forecast.

AIRA's own [search study](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554v1) reports 47.7% versus 39.6% medals on MLE-bench lite and a substantial validation–test selection gap. This supports testing candidate selection, not assuming that more search improves generalization. Those results used full DeepSeek R1/o3 and dedicated H200s; they are not a smaller-model or low-compute guarantee.

Two specific grading choices should not transfer. First, AIRS's [SolarWeekly task](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/airs-bench/main/airsbench/tasks/rad/TimeSeriesForecastingSolarWeeklyMAE/project_description.md) is an ordinary five-week forecast over 137 series, but its [scorer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/airs-bench/main/airsbench/tasks/rad/TimeSeriesForecastingSolarWeeklyMAE/evaluate.py) removes positions with NaN predictions as well as missing labels. Code inspection implies that selective missing predictions can remove difficult cases. This is an unexecuted exploit inference, not evidence that evaluated agents exploited it. Our support must be set by the controller; forecasts must be finite on every required supported case. Second, AIRS uses an observed-worst-valid score for normalization. For us, retain raw scores and fixed climatology/raw-model/reference anchors; adding a poor system must not improve another system's normalized score. Source NaNs and zero storage fills with an explicit support count are different issues.

Three concrete forecasting adaptations follow. These are proposals, not implemented tasks or demonstrated difficulty claims.

1. **Adaptive weeks3–4 rainfall correction — highest immediate value.** Reuse `weeks34-rainfall` Level 2, its genuine SubseasonalClimateUSA CFSv2/observation snapshot, existing development feedback, and the four-part reproducible envelope. Let the system choose seasonal bias correction, pooling, regularization and combinations with climatology. [The dataset paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10399) and [authors' toolkit](https://github.com/microsoft/subseasonal_toolkit) provide published meteorological/ML comparators, including adaptive bias correction and CFSv2++. Reproduce a supported comparator on the exact nine-cell source, issue dates and allowed information. A full-US paper score is not a valid target for this subset. Lock the comparator before private scoring, check improvement over both raw forecast and seasonal climatology, and assess uncertainty by year or suitably separated issue blocks. The existing source has 1194 complete issues, but overlapping two-week targets and neighbouring cells are correlated. Previously inspected final periods are development evidence; a new boundary needs a separately frozen unseen period/region. This is the closest match to the current scorer and least new infrastructure.

2. **Free OND seasonal probability design from antecedent SST.** The existing CCA source has genuine June–August SST predictors, OND CHIRPS targets and source method records. Keep the forecast issue/cutoff and ordered-probability score fixed; let the system choose regularization, dimension reduction, local versus pooled fits, probability calibration and combination. Nested or chronological training-only validation becomes necessary because more elaborate methods can overfit a short climate record. AfricaS2S supplies candidate methods; acmadDL helps with new data normalization. Reproducing the existing prescribed CCA calculation has already passed, so that is not the boundary. The four existing2020–2023 private seasons are too few and already used for prior evaluation; treat them as development only. Acquire and freeze a fresh, sufficiently long verification region/period before inference. Do not retune the failed MAM2005–2016 holdout into an apparently stronger task.

3. **Audit and improve a seasonal forecast pipeline.** Extend the existing scientific-audit question to a real executable seasonal pipeline and report: identify whether feature screening, EOF fitting, thresholds and final method selection use only permitted observations, then repair the forecast and demonstrate honest development skill. Allow different valid repairs. Use causal interventions on actual probability/forecast arrays, independent metrics and an attainable repair control; retain the original and repaired evaluations separately. This borrows RE-Bench's investigation and codebase difficulty while asking normal forecast-verification questions. A diagnostic planted fault must be labelled as constructed, rather than falsely attributed to a historical package. Method-selection independence requires more than checking one fold's final regression. Extra integration effort is warranted only if it produces a meaningful scientific question, not obscure filenames or hidden requirements.

A minimal future framework experiment can use two drafts and two improvements, with at most six controller development-score requests including invalid requests, one budget shared across all nodes, and one final candidate selected using development information only. Give a sequential condition the same backbone, packages, token limits, CPU/memory, wall budget and total scoring allowance. Reuse the current fifteen-minute/100k fresh-plus-output scale as a pilot; enlarge it only after observing useful experiments and a verified reachable reference. Do not infer from these papers that an H200 or day-long run is necessary for this compact weather problem. Keep native scientific I/O serial within each attempt and respect the existing shared two-worker limit.

The local `score_development` architecture already freezes requested artifacts and records bounded aggregate feedback. However, `assessment/runner.py:development_scorer` currently exposes hardcoded RMSE metrics and filters result variables using a `final` prefix. It is ready for the current weeks3–4 shape, not a generic probabilistic RPS task. A later RPS adaptation needs a narrow metric/output schema extension and versioned control checks; this research has not changed that code.

Before a hard-task model pilot, a scientist or trusted independent implementation must make real progress under the same source/resource constraints, and a stronger fixed comparator must leave positive headroom over trivial baselines. If that gate fails, retain the negative candidate result, as happened with MAM synthesis. During search, private final scores never enter node choice, prompts or continuation decisions. Keep all attempts and failed nodes in resource totals; report valid completion, raw scientific performance, development-to-final gap and tokens/time separately. Any approximate invoice equivalent must distinguish cached input from fresh input and remain separate from unknown subscription charges. A pass/fail anecdote remains a pilot, not a general capability boundary.
